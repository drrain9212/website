About me

Research on AI security, speech, and adversarial learning.

Dr. Rui Duan is a tenure-track Assistant Professor in the Department of Computer Science and Information Technology at the University of Missouri–Kansas City (UMKC), where he leads the Secure AI & Intelligent Media (SAIM) Lab. His research focuses on AI security, adversarial machine learning, and trustworthy, human-centered multimedia AI. His work has been supported by the National Science Foundation (NSF), including an NSF CRII Award in 2025.

Dr. Duan received his Ph.D. from the University of South Florida under the supervision of Dr. Zhuo Lu. His research has appeared in leading security and machine learning conferences, including IEEE S&P, ACM CCS, NDSS, CVPR, and ICML. His broader research interests also include network security and the Internet of Things (IoT).

ScrambleMark: Zero-Query Black-Box Evasion of Speech Watermarks

IEEE Symposium on Security and Privacy · 2027

Speech watermarks are increasingly used to identify and track audio content. ScrambleMark examines a fundamental weakness in these protections: an attacker can evade modern speech watermarking systems without knowing how the target system works or querying it. The project studies what this means for watermark robustness in practice.

Project website

Parrot-Trained Adversarial Examples

NDSS · 2024

Speaker recognition systems are often evaluated under carefully controlled conditions, but real-world attacks must contend with physical playback, recording environments, and limited knowledge of the target. This research develops parrot-trained adversarial examples and demonstrates attacks against commercial voice-enabled devices, including an over-the-air demonstration.

Perception-Aware Adversarial Audio

ACM CCS · 2022

Audio attacks are not useful if their distortions are immediately obvious to a listener. This work incorporates human auditory perception into the attack design, studying how adversarial modifications can affect audio-based machine learning systems while preserving the listening experience. The accompanying video illustrates the idea.
